This is the lecture the whole course has been quietly apologising for in advance.
Ricing is sold as a productivity project: a faster workflow, fewer keystrokes, a
machine tuned to your hand. This lecture asks whether any of that survives
contact with the arithmetic. The keybinding that saves you two seconds a day, set
up over an afternoon, pays for itself in roughly eight months, assuming you never
touch it again, which you will, on the following Tuesday.

We make the accounting honest. The customised desktop is the most elaborate
method ever devised for not doing any work, and its genius is that it feels
exactly like working: there is a terminal, there is config, there is a problem
being solved. It is procrastination wearing the clothes of productivity, and that
is precisely why it is so effective and so hard to see. The point is not to feel
guilty. It is to be able to say, clearly, what the hours bought, which is the
question the Time Audit will ask you to answer in writing.

## In this lecture

- the productivity case for ricing, stated fairly
- the arithmetic of saved seconds against spent afternoons
- procrastination that feels identical to work, and why that matters
- how to value the hours honestly, including the ones that bought only pleasure
