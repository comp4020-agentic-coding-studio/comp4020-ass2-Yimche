A window manager decides where windows go and who is allowed to move them. That
sounds like plumbing. It is closer to a constitution. A tiling manager holds
that screen space is scarce and should be allocated by rule, that the keyboard
outranks the mouse, and that the machine, not the user, should do the
arranging. A floating manager holds the opposite: that a desktop is a desk, that
overlap is natural, and that a person can be trusted to put things where they
want them.

Neither position is neutral, and this lecture treats the choice as the
ideological one it is. When you pick a window manager you are picking a theory
of work: how much you value control against convenience, ritual against
improvisation, the satisfaction of a keybinding against the ease of a drag. The
layout you end up defending in week 12 says more about you than the palette
will.

## In this lecture

- tiling, floating, and dynamic managers, and the claims each one makes
- the keyboard-first worldview and what it costs
- gaps, borders, and workspaces as expressions of a layout policy
- why the manager you choose is the hardest decision to reverse later
