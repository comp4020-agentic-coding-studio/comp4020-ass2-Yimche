Here is the turn the course has been building toward. A rice is not finished when
it works. It is finished when it has been photographed. The screenshot is the
artefact, the setup is merely the means, and once you accept that, a great deal
of otherwise irrational behaviour snaps into focus. The neofetch output posed
just so, the terminal running a command chosen for how it looks rather than what
it does, the wallpaper that peeks through gaps sized for the camera: these are
composition, and composition implies an audience.

This lecture treats the screenshot as the designed object it is. We cover framing
and what to leave out, the staged terminal and the ethics of showing a desktop
doing something it never does, and the audience the image is really for, which is
usually not the person who took it. This is also where the Defence begins. If the
screenshot is the artefact, then every choice visible in it is a claim you can be
asked to justify, and starting this week, you will be.

## In this lecture

- the screenshot as artefact, the setup as means
- composition: framing, staging, and what a good rice leaves out
- the audience, and who a desktop is really performed for
- the honest screenshot versus the one that lies about a life
