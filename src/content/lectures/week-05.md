You already know more colour theory than you use. This lecture is aimed at the
gap between the two. We cover the parts that actually govern a desktop: contrast
ratios and why your beautiful low-contrast theme is unreadable by lunchtime, hue
families and why a palette needs restraint more than range, and the difference
between a colour scheme and a colour system that survives contact with a new
application.

Then we confront the convergence. Ricing has a gravitational pull toward a
handful of palettes, and almost every setup you admire is, underneath, Gruvbox
or Catppuccin or one of their cousins. This is worth taking seriously rather than
mocking. These palettes won because they solved the real problem, legibility
across dozens of unrelated programs, better than the ones you would invent in a
weekend. The interesting question is not how to avoid them but what it means that
taste, given freedom, keeps arriving at the same six schemes.

## In this lecture

- contrast and legibility: the constraints a palette cannot negotiate away
- building a system, not a scheme, so new apps do not break it
- why Gruvbox and Catppuccin keep winning
- accessibility as a design discipline, not an afterthought

## Before next week

where the two disagree about what "background" means.
