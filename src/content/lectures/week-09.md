Ricing has two congregations and they do not worship together. The minimalists
hold that a desktop should recede, that the empty wallpaper and the single clock
are a discipline, and that everything you remove is a thing you have mastered
enough to do without. The maximalists hold that a screen is a canvas, that
density is generosity, and that a desktop crowded with widgets and colour is a
person refusing to apologise for wanting more.

This lecture treats the split as the aesthetic philosophy it is rather than a
matter of taste to be smoothed over. Minimalism and maximalism are competing
answers to the same question, which is what a desktop is for when it is not being
used. We look at the honest and dishonest versions of each: minimalism as
clarity against minimalism as fear of commitment, maximalism as abundance against
maximalism as noise standing in for a decision. You will end the course in one
church or the other, and you should know which and why.

## In this lecture

- the two positions and the claim each makes about attention
- empty space as discipline, density as generosity
- the failure modes: emptiness as avoidance, clutter as indecision
- why this is philosophy, not preference

## Before next week

Push your setup hard toward one pole for a day. Record what you gained, what you
missed, and whether you want it back.
