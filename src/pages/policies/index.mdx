# Policies and support

These rules apply to every assessment in the course. An individual brief may add
to them, but it cannot quietly relax them.

## Late work and extensions

Work submitted after its deadline loses five percentage points per day, to a
floor of zero at five days late. If something outside your control gets in the
way, ask Marisol for an extension before the deadline rather than after. A good
reason in advance is an extension; the same reason a week later is an appeal, and
appeals are harder for everyone.

## Attribution and forked configuration

Ricing runs on forked dotfiles. Copying a stranger's config, adapting it, and
making it yours is the normal way the craft is learned, and it is allowed here.
What is not allowed is passing off borrowed work as original. Every setup you
submit must credit the sources it grew from: the theme you started from, the
config you lifted a keybinding out of, the palette you did not invent. Credit
lives in your repository, in a form a marker can follow to the original.

The line the course cares about is simple. If a marker cannot tell which
decisions were yours, the work has not been documented well enough to grade, and
that is a failure of the Dotfiles Repository, not a matter for the integrity
process. If credit has been deliberately removed to make borrowed work look
original, that is the integrity process.

## AI assistance

You may use AI tools to write config, debug a compositor, or draft prose. Declare
where you did, in the same place you credit forked config. The judgement being
assessed is yours, so a setup you cannot explain is a problem whether a person or
a model produced it.

## Getting help

Bring anything about a specific piece of work to the weekly studio; that is what
it is for. For course-level questions, email Marisol. For a problem that cannot
wait until Monday, email Idris. If you are stuck for more than an hour on
something that is not the interesting part of the work, stop and ask. The hours
are being counted.
